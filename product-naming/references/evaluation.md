# Scoring and presenting

Load this at step 6 and keep it for step 7. Score only candidates that cleared the red flags in [anti-patterns.md](anti-patterns.md) and the availability gate in [availability.md](availability.md).

## Rubric

Score each criterion from 1 to 5, then multiply by its weight.

- **5.** Exceptional.
- **4.** Strong, no concerns.
- **3.** Adequate, with trade-offs.
- **2.** Weak, with real problems.
- **1.** Fails.

| Criterion | What to judge | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Metaphor | Does it plant a concrete image tied to the job? | 3 |
| Memorability | Would someone recall it tomorrow after hearing it once? | 3 |
| Story | Is there a fifteen-second origin story that gets "that's clever"? | 2 |
| Distinctiveness | Does it stand apart from competitors and the category? | 2 |
| Phone test | Can a listener spell it, and a reader say it? | 2 |
| Searchability | Use the scale in availability.md. | 2 |
| Length | One or two syllables is 5. Four or more is 1 or 2. | 1 |
| Sound | Do its sounds match the product's character? | 1 |
| Availability | How much of the must-have list is free without workarounds? | 1 |
| Longevity | Will it still work in ten years, or does it date itself? | 1 |
| Global use | Can the audience's languages say it, with no bad meanings? | 1 |
| Crowding | 5 means no other organizations nearby use it. 1 means it is established in the target industry. | 1 |
| Family fit | Does it belong beside the existing names? Drop this row for a standalone name. | 1 |

The score is the weighted sum divided by the weighted maximum of the rows used, times 100. With every row, the maximum is 105. Without family fit, it is 100.

- 80 or more is exceptional.
- 70 or more is strong.
- Below 65 is weak. If no candidate reaches 65, the territories are the problem: return to step 2.

A high total can hide a fatal weakness. A phone-test score of 2 disqualifies a name whatever its total, because the friction lasts forever.

## Sentence tests

Names live in sentences. Say every finalist in each of these, and note where it sounds wrong.

- Recommending: "Have you tried ___?" "You should try this thing called ___."
- Explaining: "We built ___ to solve [problem]." "___ is a [category] that [does what]."
- Daily use: "Check the ___ dashboard." "___ just sent an alert." "Open ___ and tap Plan."
- Marketing: "___: [tagline]." "Get started with ___ in five minutes."

Recommending is the most important test. If the name feels awkward said to a friend or colleague, word of mouth suffers.

## When scores are close

Within five points, break the tie in this order:

1. The stronger metaphor. A weak metaphor cannot be fixed later. Availability, length, and even sound can be worked around.
2. The cleaner phone test.
3. The better origin story, because it gets retold thousands of times.
4. The cleaner availability.
5. The user's preference. They live with the name.

## Presenting a finalist

Present three to five, best first. More than five stalls the decision. Fewer than three leaves nothing to compare. Use this shape for each:

```text
NAME
Story: the fifteen-second origin
Territory: where it came from, and the image it plants
Why it works: the principles it meets
Sound: how it feels said aloud
Score: N / 100
Availability (checked YYYY-MM-DD): domain ..., GitHub ..., app store ...
  Each entry says free, taken, a workaround such as getname.com, or unconfirmed.
Risks: trade-offs and anything unchecked
Taglines: one or two
Sentence: "Have you tried NAME? It [does what]."
```

A side-by-side table of the core scores helps when two finalists trade strengths:

```text
Criterion      | Name A | Name B | Name C
Metaphor       |   4    |   5    |   3
Memorability   |   5    |   4    |   4
Story          |   4    |   5    |   2
Phone test     |   5    |   4    |   2
Availability   |   3    |   4    |   5
Score          |   82   |   80   |   66
```

End with a recommendation and its reason.

## Before the user commits

Suggest sitting with the top two or three for a day. Say each aloud in different sentences, type it as if writing about the product, picture it on the home page, and tell someone and watch their face. The name that feels more right after a night's sleep usually is.

Before the decision is final, confirm:

- it has a clear origin story;
- it scored 70 or more;
- it passes the phone test;
- it is free on every must-have platform, or has an accepted workaround;
- it matches none of the anti-patterns;
- it has no bad meaning in the audience's languages;
- someone outside the naming work liked it on first hearing.
