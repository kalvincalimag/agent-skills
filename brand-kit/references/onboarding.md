# Mascot story and first greeting

The first minute should make people like the character and land them in the real product. It should not teach them the product. If a screen needs a tour to be understood, fix the screen.

## What to take from character-led apps

- **A short wordless story.** The mascot acts out two to four beats on a quiet stage, a plain dark or light background with nothing else, before any feature appears. It shows personality without a single instruction.
- **Tap anywhere to continue,** with a small hint, and **Skip** always visible in the same corner.
- **A greeting in the real product.** On the first real screen, the mascot says hello once, by its name, in one or two short lines, beside its approved greeting pose.
- **The character keeps pointing at the brand.** The mark, or the mascot's defining trait, appears in the story, so icon and character keep reinforcing each other.

## What to skip

- **Coach marks and tours.** Dimmed screens with a card per tab explain the interface instead of letting it speak. Leave them out when the product's rule is that good screens need no tour. If the user does want them, allow one card per main area at most, each dismissible, with no forced sequence.
- **A reference's story beats.** If an admired app's mascot wakes up, stretches and cheers, a mascot doing the same reads as its clone. Tell the product's own story, from its origin and subject.
- **Sign-up, permissions or a paywall inside the story.** Ask for them later, at the moment they are needed.

## Writing the story

1. Start from the product's origin or main job, in one sentence, such as "a feast is laid out and the character invites you to it".
2. Break it into two to four beats. Each beat is one approved pose and one change in the scene, readable in about a second without words.
3. Use only the mascot's approved poses. If a beat needs a new pose, make it from the master the way the mascot's poses are made, and let the user approve it.
4. End on the real product with real or sample content, not an empty screen. The last beat hands over to the first screen the user will actually use.
5. Count the taps from opening the app to the real product. Keep it to four or fewer, and one with Skip.

## Accessibility

- Every beat has a text description for screen readers, and Skip is reachable first.
- With reduced motion on, show each beat as a still, or skip straight to the greeting.
- Nothing in the story depends on sound or colour alone.

## Checks

- The story is two to four beats, wordless, skippable, and built from approved poses.
- It tells the product's own story and copies no reference's beats.
- It ends on real or sample content, and the mascot greets the user once, by name.
- No sign-up, permission request or paywall appears before the real product.
