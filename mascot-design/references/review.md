# Mascot review

Run this before presenting candidates, and again after every change to them. The goal is to find evidence that a candidate is weaker, more generic, or less alive than the reference characters. Do not try to confirm the designer's choice.

## The blind lineup

This is the main test. Legibility checks cannot replace it.

1. Build two lineup images and save them as files. The hero lineup puts each candidate's hero art beside the references' richest art. The product lineup puts each candidate's in-product vector beside the references' flat in-product art. In both, use the same size, the same canvas, and no names or labels, and shuffle the order. Prepare references and candidates the same way, with the same crop, resolution, and background, so differences in image tools do not give the drafts away.
2. Give the lineup files to a fresh reviewer who did not design the candidates and has not seen the designer's notes. A subagent with fresh context counts. Tell them that some characters come from shipped products and the rest are drafts, without saying how many of each.
3. Ask the reviewer to:
   - describe each character in one sentence, from the image alone;
   - name each character's personality in three words;
   - rank all characters by how memorable and finished they look;
   - point out anything that looks generic, unfinished, stiff, or like clip art;
   - pick which characters are the drafts, and say what gave each one away. Only design reasons count, such as shape, face, pose, or concept. Rendering differences such as texture, resolution, or line quality from a different tool do not;
   - say whether any character looks like a copy of another.
4. Fail a candidate when the reviewer picks it as a draft for a craft reason, such as stiffness, clip-art construction, or a generic face, when its one-sentence description is generic, when its personality words are generic, such as cute, friendly, or happy, when it ranks clearly below every reference, or when it looks like a copy of a reference.

Use the reference characters found in step 2 of the skill: ones the user named, or ones recorded in the project's notes. If there are none, use two widely known product mascots, and say which.

## Character checks

- **Face covered.** Cover the face. Fail if the body, trait, costume, and pose say nothing about who it is.
- **Subject tie.** Fail if a viewer told what the product does cannot connect the character to it.
- **Voice.** Read its sample lines aloud. Fail if the lines could belong to any app.
- **Distinct faces.** Fail the board if two candidates share a face construction.
- **Acting.** Fail if any acting pose has a floating prop, a straight front-facing body, or a change in the face only.
- **Staging.** Fail if the candidate is shown only on a blank board and never inside the product or a scene.

## Legibility checks

- **Silhouette.** Render the full-body in-product vector as a solid black fill at 24 and 48 px, and the simplified icon version at 16 px. Render the references the same way. Fail if a fresh viewer cannot tell a candidate from a generic blob or from the other candidates, unless the references fail at that size too.
- **Expression.** View five moods at 48 px. Fail if two moods cannot be told apart.
- **Contrast.** Place it on the light canvas and on a dark tile. Fail if either placement needs a backing plate.
- **Vector tier.** Fail if the in-product version uses gradients, shading, or detail that disappears at 24 px.
- **Rig-ready.** Fail if the construction cannot be split into movable parts, for example when the limbs or head merge into one shape with the body. The rig itself is built and checked after the user picks.

## Record and act

Record every result next to the candidate on the board. A candidate that fails any check cannot be the recommended one. Fix supported failures and repeat the lineup with a new fresh reviewer, since the previous reviewer has already seen the candidates. After two improvement rounds, present any remaining failure plainly instead of lowering the bar.
