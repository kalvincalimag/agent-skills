# Mascot checks

Run these before presenting candidates, and again after every change to them. The checks find measurable problems, the evidence that a candidate is weaker, more generic, or less consistent than it should be. They do not decide whether a character is appealing or ready. The user judges that by looking at the lineup.

## The lineup

Build two lineup images and save them as files. The hero lineup puts each candidate's hero art beside the references' richest art. The product lineup puts each candidate's in-product art beside the references' in-product art. In both, use the same size, the same canvas, and the same crop, resolution, and background for every character, so differences in image tools do not decide the comparison. Show both to the user, who judges whether each candidate looks as finished, as specific, and as alive as the references.

Use the reference characters found in step 2 of the skill: ones the user named, or ones recorded in the project's notes. If there are none, use two widely known product mascots, and say which.

## Reviewer checks

Give the lineup files and the checks below to fresh reviewers. None of them designed the candidates or has seen the designer's notes. A subagent with fresh context counts. Give every reviewer the same instructions, and keep the checks unchanged across rounds so results stay comparable.

Each check passes or fails, with evidence. Reviewers report failures and do not prescribe redesigns. They do not rank characters, guess which ones are drafts, or give a ready verdict. When a reviewer suggests a new design, treat it as a note, not an instruction.

### Character checks

- **Description.** Describe each character in one sentence from the image alone. Fail if the sentence is generic, such as "a cute orange animal".
- **Face covered.** Cover the face. Fail if the body, trait, costume, and pose say nothing about who it is.
- **Subject tie.** Fail if a viewer told what the product does cannot connect the character to it.
- **Voice.** Read its sample lines aloud. Fail if the lines could belong to any app.
- **Distinct faces.** Fail the board if two candidates share a face construction.
- **Acting.** Fail if any acting pose has a floating prop, a straight front-facing body, or a change in the face only.
- **Staging.** Fail if the candidate is shown only on a blank board and never inside the product or a scene.
- **Lookalike.** Fail if the character reads as a known character or mascot, or very close to one. Name it.

### Consistency checks

- **Drift.** Compare every pose with the hero, or with the approved master when one exists. Fail if the face, the signature trait, the proportions, or the colors changed.

### Legibility checks

- **Silhouette.** Render the full-body in-product art as a solid black fill at 24 and 48 px, and the simplified version at 16 px. Render the references the same way. Fail if a fresh viewer cannot tell a candidate from a generic blob or from the other candidates, unless the references fail at that size too.
- **Expression.** View five moods at 48 px. Fail if two moods cannot be told apart.
- **Contrast.** Place it on every surface the product uses, including the light canvas, brand-colored tiles, and dark mode. Fail if any placement needs a backing plate, or if a signature part blends into a surface of the same color.
- **In-product tier.** Fail if the simplified version does not read at 16 px. For a flat style, also fail gradients, shading, or detail that disappears at 24 px.
- **Rig-ready.** For vector art only: fail if the construction cannot be split into movable parts, for example when the limbs or head merge into one shape with the body. The rig itself is built and checked after the user picks.

## Record and act

Record every result next to the candidate on the board.

- **Fix failures.** Fix a failed check inside the area under revision. Record failures outside it as open items, and do not act on them.
- **Agreement.** When several reviewers ran the same checks, act on a failure that at least two raise. A failure from one reviewer goes on the board as a note.
- **Locks.** Never change a locked trait because of a check. When a failure can be fixed only by changing a locked trait, report it and let the user decide.
- **Repeat.** After fixing, run the checks again with fresh reviewers, since the earlier ones have already seen the candidates.
- **The user decides.** The user picks between candidates and between options, and says when a character is ready. When the user accepts a character that fails a check, record the failure as a note and follow the user.
