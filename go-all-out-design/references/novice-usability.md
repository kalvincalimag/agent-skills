# Beginner usability check

Run this check before visual direction work and again before presenting the direction for approval. The direction must stand on its own. It cannot depend on a previous direction or the designer's explanation.

The reviewer must not know the design rationale. Hide annotations, concept names, and explanatory notes, and show only what a real user would see. If a fresh human reviewer is unavailable, use a reviewer with fresh context and no access to the design notes, and record that limitation. Do not claim the product is proven intuitive until a suitable person completes the test.

## First look

Show the first useful screen for five seconds, then hide it. Ask:

1. What does this product help you do?
2. What would you do first?
3. What do you expect to happen next?

Fail the check if the reviewer needs a spoken explanation, mistakes decoration for the main action, or cannot name the next step.

## First use without help

- **No tours or coach marks.** If a first screen needs a guided tour, arrows or a step guide to be understood, the screen fails. Fix the screen.
- **Open the real product, already filled.** The first open lands on the same home screen and navigation a returning user has. It is filled with a clearly labelled sample, so the person sees what a finished result looks like. Offer Keep or Start empty, and change nothing real until Keep.
- **Ask for an account when saving matters.** Unless the requirements need it earlier, ask the first time the person saves something worth keeping. Ask softly, with a way to decline, and never before they have seen value.

Fail the first look if the reviewer can only explain the product after reading a tour or a guide.

## Main task

Give the reviewer one goal in everyday language. Do not tell them which control to use. Watch for:

- hesitation about where to start
- labels they do not understand
- choices that appear before they have enough information
- hidden gestures or controls
- uncertainty about whether an action worked
- dead ends or a need for coaching

The task passes when the reviewer reaches the intended result, understands what changed, and can say what they would do next.

## Mistake and recovery

Ask the reviewer to change, undo, cancel, or recover from one realistic mistake. Recovery passes when they find it, understand its effect, and return to a safe state without help.

## Content check

Read every visible sentence as someone who has never used this type of product.

- Use short, everyday words.
- Explain a necessary new term when it first appears.
- Name actions by their result.
- Remove text that does not help a decision or action.
- Do not rely on icons, color, or illustrations alone for essential meaning.
