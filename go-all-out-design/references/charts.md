# Charts

On a resting screen, prefer the one number that drives the next decision. Draw a chart only when a trend is the point, such as progress over a week or weight over months. Then make it sleek and quiet.

## Bars

- Rounded, capsule-shaped bars, each on a light track that shows the full scale.
- One highlighted bar, the latest or chosen value, in the strong colour with its value in a small label. The other bars are a lighter tint of the same colour.
- A goal as a thin dashed line with its value in a small label at one end.
- Partial data, such as a day with some values unknown, as a striped bar. Missing data, such as a day with nothing logged, as an empty track. Give both a key, because colour alone must not carry the meaning.
- Single-letter or short labels under the bars, and no axis lines, ticks or gridlines.

## Lines

- A smooth curve, not straight segments, with a soft fill under it that fades to transparent.
- Only the latest point is marked, with its value in a small label. Dates appear only at the two ends.

## Around the chart

- Say what the chart measures and in what units, in its title or its one-line summary, such as "Average 1,980 a day".
- A switch between measures is a segmented control. A range switch, such as week or month, sits beside it.
- Every value is available as text to screen readers, and the latest value is also shown as a number.
- Charts do not redraw from zero on every visit; see [aliveness.md](aliveness.md).

## Avoid

- Stacked bars, several rings or gauges, and 3D effects.
- Flat dark bars at full height on a white card with a heavy axis.
- Showing a missing day as zero.
- Smoothing that hides a real dip or spike.
